Community Impact

Why Mapping Daladalas Matters

Daladalas are the heartbeat of public mobility across Unguja. By contributing your rides, you help bridge the transit information divide for all Zanzibaris.

Close the Transit Data Gap

Most Daladala corridors have never had published routes, schedules, or verified stops. ZanNav Mapper transforms informal, word-of-mouth routes into structured geographic knowledge.

Empower Everyday Commuters

When you map a route, you help students travelling to SUZA, hospital workers heading to Mnazi Mmoja, and island residents navigating cross-street transfers safely with zero guesswork.

100% Open Data & OSM Standards

Your field survey submissions feed directly into OpenStreetMap and open transit datasets under the ODbL license, freely accessible to urban researchers, students, and software developers.

Every Single Ride Counts

No specialized survey equipment or cartography degrees required. If you ride Daladalas regularly, you can log high-precision GPS route paths directly from your Android smartphone.

Contributor Guide

How to Map Daladalas in 4 Steps

Turn your daily Daladala commute into an open transit contribution with ZanNav Mapper.

01

Get ZanNav Mapper

On Android, download the v1.0.6 APK directly from GitHub. On iPhone / iPad, launch the dedicated Web App in Safari (and tap "Add to Home Screen"). 100% free with zero registration required.

02

Board & Start GPS Tracing

Hop into your Daladala at any terminal (Mnazi Mmoja, Darajani, etc.). Tap "Start Survey" and keep your phone near a window or pocket to record GPS breadcrumbs.

03

Pin Bus Stops (Vituo)

Whenever the Daladala stops to pick up or drop passengers, tap "Add Stop". Enter its local Swahili name, note if there's a shelter or sign, and flag transfer points.

04

Submit & Open The Data

Upon arriving at the terminus, tap "Finish Survey". Review your route polyline and stop sequence, then submit or export the spatial dataset to the review pipeline.

Ready to map on your next trip?

Whether you're riding Line 504 to Fuoni, commuting down to Kiembe Samaki, or heading to Bububu, your GPS survey makes public transport better for everyone across Zanzibar.

Field-Grade Capabilities

Engineered for Island Realities

Designed from the ground up to solve the real challenges of informal transit data collection in Unguja.

100% Offline Field Collection

Map rural or coastal Daladala routes across Unguja with zero cellular data. All GPS logs and stop attributes save securely on-device until you connect to Wi-Fi.

Real-Time GPS Route Logging

Adaptive GPS breadcrumbs capture the exact roadway trajectory of moving Daladalas, filtering vehicle drift while keeping battery consumption exceptionally low.

One-Tap Bus Stop Marking

Quickly drop stops at verified vituo. Tag local Swahili names, travel directions (inbound/outbound), shelter presence, and roadside landmarks without slowing your ride.

Terminals & Transfer Junctions

Explicitly identify central interchange hubs (Mnazi Mmoja, Darajani, Mwanakwerekwe) and cross-street transfer nodes where passengers switch routes.

Export to Open Standards

Export completed surveys directly into GeoJSON, GPX, and OSM transit relation formats for seamless integration into OpenStreetMap, JOSM, or QGIS workflows.

Android APK & iOS Web App

Survey Daladalas on any phone. Native Android APK (v1.0.6) with background GPS services, plus an instant Safari Progressive Web App (PWA) tailored for iPhone and iPad users.

No. Both the Android native app and the iOS Progressive Web App are built offline-first. You can start a survey, log high-resolution GPS tracks, and drop bus stop pins with zero internet connection or cellular mobile balance. All data is cached on your device. Once you are home or connected to Wi-Fi, you can review and submit your survey with one tap.
For best GPS satellite reception, sit near an open window and hold your phone or place it upright in your pocket. Avoid covering the top edge of your phone. ZanNav Mapper incorporates an on-device jitter filter that eliminates erratic location jumps and cleans up road geometry automatically.
Always use the recognized local Swahili name called out by conductors (makondakta) and passengers—for example, "Kisimani Mwisho", "Makada", "Mwanakwerekwe Sokoni", or "Darajani Stand". If there is an official municipal sign, prominent landmark (e.g., msikiti, hospitali, or shule), include it in the stop notes!
Submitted surveys go through a community validation review to reconcile stop coordinates, clean corridor paths, and verify terminus pairs. The verified data is added to OpenStreetMap (OSM) public transit relations and exported as open GTFS feeds. This directly updates the offline transit map used by thousands of island commuters.
